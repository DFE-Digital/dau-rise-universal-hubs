# Engagement — RISE Universal

## Introduction

This document describes the engagement model used to plan, develop, and maintain the **RISE Universal** data, reporting, and digital tooling.

The model ensures DAU and policy colleagues collaborate in a structured, transparent, and predictable way, with full traceability of decisions and changes through GitHub.

It applies to all non‑trivial updates to logic, data models, workflows, or user experience.

---

## TL;DR

### Purpose
A consistent engagement framework for **RISE Universal** updates — ensuring clear communication, predictable delivery, and auditable decision‑making.

### Regular Engagement
- **Monthly Data & Tools Meeting**

### Update Cycle
- Start‑of‑Cycle Kick‑off  
- Mid‑Cycle Show & Tell  
- Pre‑Launch Review  

### After Release
- Retrospective

---

## Regular Engagement

### Monthly Data & Tools Meeting

A standing monthly meeting bringing together DAU and policy colleagues to:

- Share upcoming work, policy changes, and dependencies  
- Surface risks early  
- Review tool usage and data quality  
- Identify opportunities for incremental improvements  
- Maintain alignment across teams  

**Outputs**
- New ideas, risks, and requests logged as GitHub issues  
- Agreed follow‑up actions with named owners  
- Escalations recorded where required  

---

## Update Cycle Engagement

### 1. Start‑of‑Cycle Kick‑off

A structured discussion at the beginning of each update cycle to:

- Confirm scope and requirements  
- Explore design and implementation options  
- Identify technical or data risks  
- Agree delivery expectations and timelines  
- Clarify roles and decision‑making responsibilities  

**Outputs**
- Confirmed scope and feature list  
- GitHub issues created for all work items  
- Shared delivery plan  
- Named owners for each task  
- Early risks documented  

---

### 2. Mid‑Cycle Show & Tell

A mid‑cycle checkpoint to:

- Demonstrate in‑progress functionality  
- Gather early feedback  
- Agree small adjustments suitable for in‑cycle delivery  
- Log major changes as future work  
- Validate delivery remains on track  

**Outputs**
- Minor changes added to the current milestone  
- Larger requests logged for future cycles  
- Updated priorities and sequencing  
- Feedback captured and actions assigned  

---

### 3. Pre‑Launch Review

A near‑final walkthrough to:

- Review the end‑to‑end user experience  
- Confirm data quality and validation outcomes  
- Capture final feedback  
- Agree last‑minute fixes or deferrals  
- Provide assurance prior to release  

**Outputs**
- Final fixes confirmed  
- Deferred work logged  
- Sign‑off recorded  
- Release notes drafted  
- Launch plan agreed  

---

## Retrospective

A retrospective is held after each release cycle to support continuous improvement.

### Purpose
- Celebrate successes  
- Identify areas for improvement  
- Understand root causes of issues  
- Capture actionable improvements  

---

### Structure

1. **What Went Well**  
2. **What Could Be Improved**  
3. **Risks, Dependencies & Blockers**  
4. **Actionable Improvements**  
5. **Future Ideas & Opportunities**

---

### Outputs
- Improvements logged as GitHub issues  
- Updated priorities for future cycles  
- Summary shared with stakeholders  
- Actions and owners agreed
